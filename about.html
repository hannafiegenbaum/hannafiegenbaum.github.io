About

What am I working on? I work on and research integration of sustainability indicators, decisions and processes into business and finance. During the last years, this has included sustainable project finance and application of the mitigation hierarchy, integrating multiple sustainability objectives and KPIs such as circular materials, energy efficiency, material choices, biodiversity strategy and carbon reduction measures into real estate development, mitigating trade-offs between climate and nature-related objectives, and advancing integration of nature- and climate-related goals into business and financial decision-making. Recent work focuses on nature policy and finance, environmental markets and enabling nature-related decision making. More currently, I am working on translating this work into model-based applications, researching mechanism choice and design for ecosystem restoration as well as their performance over time and how they fit different stakeholder preferences and their contributions.

How did I get there? The short answer is – values and decision. I grew up in the countryside in Germany and spending time in nature, but also managed forests, forestry and agriculture were part of my everyday childhood life. Preserving these valuable natural resources was never only a conservation but also, and even more so, an economic decision. The long answer is – I was a great sampler when I was a student. I have taken classes in economics, operations research, computational neuroscience, art history, French literature, fine art, the social sciences, while studying psychology, applied computer science and philosophy. I took a logic class in three different disciplines – philosophy, maths and computer science – to understand the differences in how it is taught across subjects and to get closer to achieving a more complete overview of the subject matter. The same, over the years, has become clear to me about causal inference – it makes a difference whether you come from an econometrics, conservation, computer science, clinical trial, philosophy, or statistics background in how approaches to inferring causality have developed across fields. But partly also because the way how causation is embedded or applied, differs. At the core of this was mostly a methodological curiosity – what methods are applicable across domains and why. You should not be able to infer an effect of x on y from observations if the relation of x and y does not involve causality. What else am I interested in? I enjoy running and hiking, watching football, going to fine art shows, and occassionally writing about them, too. I listen to an unhealthy amount of music almost every day. I have taught Germany classes on the side during the last ten years when I had the time and I have never gotten tired of learning from my students about their countries of origin or countries of choice, culture, languages, traditions, food, and ecosystems.
