About

What am I working on?

I work on and research the integration of sustainability indicators, decisions and processes into business and finance, with a particular focus on nature-related decisions.

Over the last years, this has included:

  • Sustainable project finance and the application of the mitigation hierarchy
  • Integrating multiple sustainability objectives and KPIs — such as reuse or circularity of materials, energy efficiency, material choices, biodiversity strategies and carbon reduction measures — into real estate development
  • Mitigating trade-offs between climate- and nature-related objectives
  • Advancing the integration of nature- and climate-related goals into business and financial decision-making
  • Nature and carbon credit policy and environmental markets

More recently, I have been working on translating this work into formal, quantitative, model-based applications, researching mechanism choice and design for ecosystem restoration, their performance over time, and how different mechanisms fit stakeholder preferences and contributions.

How did I get there?

The short answer is: values and decisions. I grew up in the countryside in Germany, where spending time in nature — but also in managed forests and agricultural landscapes — was part of my everyday childhood and teenage life. Preserving these valuable natural resources was never only a conservation question, but also, and perhaps even more importantly, an economic one.

The long answer is: After being a huge sampler as a student – I took classes in economics, statistics, operations research, the social sciences, computational neuroscience, art history, French literature, fine art and social and political theory, while studying psychology, applied computer science and philosophy – my interests converged around methodological range: which methods are applicable in particular contexts or systems and why? Further, what types of inference exist in different domains, how may they interdepend and, if so, how are they learned or how do they materialize? What conditions make different epistemic types of inference internally or externally valid? Where do methodological choices reflect domain features and where are they mostly driven by epistemic processes?

Nature finance, sustainable business and ecological economics are fields in which much of this methodological expertise can not only be applied, but also put to the purpose of changing how humans interact with their environment, natural resources and biodiversity — towards relationships that are less harmful and distribute benefits more equally across species.

What else am I interested in?

I enjoy running and hiking, watching football, going to fine art shows, and occasionally writing about them too. I listen to an unhealthy amount of music. I have taught German classes on the side over the last ten years whenever I had the time, and I have never grown tired of learning from my students about their countries of origin or countries of choice, their cultures, languages, traditions, food and ecosystems.
